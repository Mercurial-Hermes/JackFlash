# Year 7 Science: Keeping Afloat (8.5)

## About This Topic

Students distinguish between buoyancy and surface tension and connect both ideas to everyday examples of floating.

## Section 1: Buoyancy

**Buoyancy** is the upward force a fluid (liquid or gas) exerts on an object.

An object floats when buoyancy is equal to or greater than its weight.
An object sinks when weight is greater than buoyancy.

Q: What is buoyancy?
A: The upward force from a fluid on an object.

Q: When does an object float?
A: When the buoyant force is equal to or greater than the object's weight.

## Section 2: Surface Tension

**Surface tension** is caused by attractive forces between liquid particles at the surface.
It makes the surface behave like a thin elastic skin.

Q: What is surface tension?
A: A property of liquid surfaces caused by particle attraction, making the surface resist breaking.

Q: Name an example of surface tension.
A: Water striders walking on water, or a paper clip floating carefully on water.

## Section 3: Buoyancy vs Surface Tension

Both can help objects stay up, but they are different:
- buoyancy acts through the fluid on submerged or floating objects
- surface tension acts at the liquid surface

Q: What is one key difference between buoyancy and surface tension?
A: Buoyancy is an upward fluid force on volume; surface tension is a surface effect at the liquid boundary.
