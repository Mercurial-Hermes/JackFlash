# Year 7 Science: Friction (8.4)

## About This Topic

Students describe friction as a force that opposes motion, identify when friction is useful or a nuisance, and explain how engineers design with friction in mind.

## Section 1: Friction Opposes Motion

**Friction** is a contact force that opposes relative motion between surfaces.

Types include:
- static friction (prevents sliding)
- sliding friction
- rolling friction
- fluid friction (air resistance, water resistance)

Q: What is friction?
A: A contact force that opposes motion between surfaces.

Q: In which direction does friction act?
A: Opposite to the direction of motion (or attempted motion).

## Section 2: Useful Friction and Nuisance Friction

Friction can be useful:
- walking without slipping
- braking a bike or car
- writing with pencil on paper

Friction can also be a nuisance:
- wear in machine parts
- heat and energy loss
- harder movement of objects

Q: Give one example where friction is useful.
A: Example: friction between shoes and ground helps us walk.

Q: Give one example where friction is a nuisance.
A: Example: friction in engines causes heat and wear.

## Section 3: Designing with Friction

Engineers control friction depending on the goal:
- increase friction: rough surfaces, tread patterns, grip materials
- decrease friction: lubricants, smooth surfaces, ball bearings

Q: How can friction be reduced in machines?
A: By using lubricants, smoother surfaces, or ball bearings.

Q: Why is understanding friction important in design?
A: Designers need to increase friction for grip and safety, or reduce it for efficiency and less wear.
