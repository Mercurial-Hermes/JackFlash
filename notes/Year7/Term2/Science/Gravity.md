# Year 7 Science: Gravity (8.3)

## About This Topic

Students explain gravity, distinguish between mass and weight, and investigate how falling motion is affected by gravity and air resistance.

## Section 1: What Is Gravity?

**Gravity** is a non-contact force that attracts objects with mass toward each other.
On Earth, gravity pulls objects toward the center of the planet.

Q: What is gravity?
A: A non-contact force that attracts objects with mass toward each other.

Q: Why do objects fall to the ground on Earth?
A: Because Earth's gravity pulls them toward Earth's center.

## Section 2: Mass and Weight

**Mass** is how much matter is in an object (measured in kilograms, kg).
**Weight** is the force of gravity acting on that mass (measured in newtons, N).

Mass stays the same in different places, but weight changes if gravity changes.

Q: What is the difference between mass and weight?
A: Mass is amount of matter (kg); weight is gravitational force on that mass (N).

Q: Does your mass change on the Moon?
A: No, mass stays the same.

Q: Does your weight change on the Moon?
A: Yes, weight is less because gravity is weaker than on Earth.

## Section 3: Falling Objects and Air Resistance

When objects fall, gravity pulls down while **air resistance** pushes up.

- If gravity is greater than air resistance, the object speeds up.
- As speed increases, air resistance increases.
- Eventually forces can balance and the object falls at constant speed (terminal velocity).

Q: What two forces act on a falling object?
A: Gravity downward and air resistance upward.

Q: Why does a parachute make a skydiver fall more slowly?
A: It increases air resistance, reducing acceleration and lowering terminal speed.

Q: What happens when gravity and air resistance are balanced?
A: The object falls at constant speed (no acceleration).
