# Year 7 Science: Electric Fields (8.7)

## About This Topic

Students explain charging, electrostatic forces, natural events like lightning and shocks, and practical uses of static electricity.

## Section 1: How Objects Become Charged

Objects can become charged when electrons move between materials.
This can happen by rubbing, contact, or induction.

- gaining electrons gives negative charge
- losing electrons gives positive charge

Q: How can an object become electrically charged?
A: By transfer of electrons, such as rubbing, contact, or induction.

Q: What happens when an object gains electrons?
A: It becomes negatively charged.

## Section 2: Forces Between Charged Objects

Electrostatic forces are non-contact forces.
- like charges repel
- unlike charges attract
- charged objects can attract some uncharged objects by inducing charge separation

Q: What happens between two positively charged objects?
A: They repel each other.

Q: Can a charged object attract an uncharged object?
A: Yes, by inducing charge separation in the uncharged object.

## Section 3: Lightning and Electrostatic Shocks

Charge can build up in clouds or on objects.
When the electric field becomes strong enough, charge suddenly discharges.

Examples:
- lightning between cloud and ground
- static shock when touching metal after walking on carpet

Q: What causes lightning?
A: A sudden discharge of built-up electric charge when the electric field is strong enough.

Q: Why might you get a static shock after walking on carpet?
A: Charge builds on your body and discharges when you touch a conductor.

## Section 4: Uses of Static Electricity

Useful applications include:
- electrostatic paint spraying
- photocopiers and laser printers
- electrostatic precipitators that remove smoke particles

Q: Give one useful application of static electricity.
A: Example: electrostatic paint spraying or dust removal in smokestacks.
