# Year 7 Science: Magnetic Fields (8.6)

## About This Topic

Students identify magnetic and non-magnetic materials, describe magnetic forces, represent fields, use compasses, and explain electromagnets and their uses.

## Section 1: Magnetic and Non-Magnetic Substances

Some substances are attracted strongly by magnets (for example iron, nickel, cobalt). Others are not.

Q: Name one magnetic substance.
A: Iron, nickel, cobalt, or steel.

Q: Name one non-magnetic substance.
A: Plastic, wood, glass, aluminum, or rubber.

## Section 2: Forces Between Magnets and Objects

Magnets have north and south poles.
- unlike poles attract
- like poles repel

Magnets can attract magnetic objects without touching them.

Q: What happens between two north poles?
A: They repel.

Q: What happens between a north and south pole?
A: They attract.

## Section 3: Representing Magnetic Fields

A **magnetic field** is the region where magnetic forces act.
Field lines:
- go from north to south outside a magnet
- are closer together where field is stronger

Earth has a magnetic field similar to a giant bar magnet.

Q: What does a magnetic field line diagram show?
A: The direction and relative strength of the magnetic field.

Q: How can a compass show field direction?
A: The compass needle aligns with the magnetic field direction.

## Section 4: Electromagnets

An **electromagnet** is made by passing current through a coil of wire, often around an iron core.
Its magnetic field can be turned on and off.

Uses include:
- scrapyard cranes
- electric bells
- relays and motors
- MRI and speakers (advanced examples)

Q: How does an electromagnet work?
A: Electric current in a coil creates a magnetic field, often strengthened by an iron core.

Q: Give one use of an electromagnet.
A: Example: a scrapyard crane lifting metal.
