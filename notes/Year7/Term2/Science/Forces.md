# Year 7 Science: Forces (8.2)

## About This Topic

In this topic, students identify and describe how forces change motion. They learn to classify forces as contact or non-contact, represent forces with arrows, and recognize balanced and unbalanced situations.

## Section 1: Forces and Motion

A **force** is a push or pull that can change the motion of an object.

Forces can:
- speed an object up
- slow an object down
- change the direction of motion
- change shape

Q: What is a force?
A: A push or pull that can change an object's motion or shape.

Q: Name two ways a force can change motion.
A: Any two of: speed up, slow down, change direction, start motion, stop motion.

## Section 2: Contact and Non-Contact Forces

**Contact forces** act when objects are touching.
Examples: friction, air resistance, normal force, tension.

**Non-contact forces** act at a distance.
Examples: gravity, magnetic force, electric force.

Q: What is the difference between a contact and a non-contact force?
A: Contact forces need touching objects; non-contact forces act at a distance.

Q: Give one contact force and one non-contact force.
A: Example: friction (contact) and gravity (non-contact).

## Section 3: Force Arrows and Balanced Forces

Forces can be shown on diagrams using **arrows**:
- arrow direction shows force direction
- arrow length shows force size
- arrows are drawn from the object

If forces are equal and opposite, they are **balanced** and the object has no acceleration.
If not equal, they are **unbalanced** and the object accelerates.

Q: What do force arrows show in a diagram?
A: The direction and size of forces acting on an object.

Q: What happens when forces on an object are balanced?
A: The object does not accelerate; it stays still or moves at constant speed in a straight line.

Q: What happens when forces are unbalanced?
A: The object accelerates in the direction of the net force.
